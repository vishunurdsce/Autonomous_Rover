# Case Study: NASA Mars Perseverance Rover - Technical Details

This document provides raw details and findings from the case study of NASA's Mars 2020 mission, focusing on the Perseverance Rover.

## 1. Mission Overview
- **Mission Name:** Mars 2020
- **Rover Name:** Perseverance (Percy)
- **Launch Date:** July 30, 2020
- **Landing Date:** February 18, 2021
- **Landing Site:** Jezero Crater (coordinates: 18.44°N 77.45°E)
- **Primary Goal:** Astrobiology, including the search for signs of ancient microbial life.

## 2. Core Scientific Objectives
1. **Habitability:** Identify past environments capable of supporting microbial life.
2. **Biosignatures:** Search for signs of ancient microbial life in rocks known to preserve them.
3. **Sample Caching:** Collect rock and regolith samples and "cache" them on the surface for future return to Earth.
4. **Prepare for Humans:** Test technologies that could support future human exploration (e.g., oxygen production).

## 3. Scientific Payload (The Seven Instruments)
- **Mastcam-Z:** High-resolution panoramic and stereoscopic cameras with zoom capability.
- **SuperCam:** Remote sensing instrument using laser-induced breakdown spectroscopy (LIBS) for chemical analysis.
- **PIXL:** Planetary Instrument for X-ray Lithochemistry; maps fine-scale elemental composition.
- **SHERLOC:** Scanning Habitable Environments with Raman & Luminescence for Organics & Chemicals; UV laser for organic detection.
- **RIMFAX:** Radar Imager for Mars' Subsurface Experiment; ground-penetrating radar.
- **MEDA:** Mars Environmental Dynamics Analyzer; weather and dust sensors.
- **MOXIE:** Mars Oxygen In-Situ Resource Utilization Experiment; successfully produced oxygen from Martian CO2.

## 4. Sampling and Caching System
- **Robotic Arm:** 2.1 meters (7 feet) long with a percussive drill.
- **Sample Selection:** Uses PIXL and SHERLOC to identify high-value targets.
- **Sample Tubes:** 43 titanium tubes designed to stay hermetically sealed.
- **Sample Depot:** The rover has already deposited a "backup" cache of 10 tubes at a site called "Three Forks" in Jezero Crater.

## 5. Technology Demonstrations
- **Ingenuity Mars Helicopter:** 
    - First powered flight on another planet.
    - Completed 72 flights (mission ended in Jan 2024).
    - Served as a scout for the rover.
- **Terrain Relative Navigation (TRN):** Autonomous landing system that avoided hazards during descent.
- **AutoNav:** Advanced autonomous driving software allowing the rover to navigate around obstacles without constant human input.

## 6. Key Discoveries to Date
- **Ancient Delta:** Confirmed the presence of an ancient river delta in Jezero Crater, signifying long-term water stability.
- **Igneous Rocks:** Found that the crater floor is primarily volcanic in origin, providing a timeline for Martian geological history.
- **Organic Molecules:** Detection of complex carbon-based molecules in Plusieurs rock samples (Jezero Crater delta).
- **Atmospheric Oxygen:** MOXIE demonstrated oxygen production 16 times, totaling 122 grams of oxygen.

## 7. Future Mission: Mars Sample Return (MSR)
- Joint NASA-ESA mission planned for the 2030s.
- Involves a Sample Retrieval Lander, a Mars Ascent Vehicle, and an Earth Return Orbiter.
